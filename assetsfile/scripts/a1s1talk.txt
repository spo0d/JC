SAY:FLAVIUS:Hence! Home, you idle creatures get you home:|
SAY:FLAVIUS:Is this a holiday? What, know you not,|
SAY:FLAVIUS:Being mechanical, you ought not walk|
SAY:FLAVIUS:Upon a laboring day without the sign|
SAY:FLAVIUS:Of your profession? Speak, what trade art thou?|
SAY:CARPENTER:Why, sir, a carpenter.|
SAY:MURELLUS:Where is thy leather apron and thy rule?|
SAY:MURELLUS:What dost thou with thy best apparel on? —|
SAY:MURELLUS:You, sir, what trade are you?|
SAY:COBBLER:Truly, sir, in respect of a fine workman, I am but,|
SAY:COBBLER:as you would say, a cobbler.|
SAY:MURELLUS:But what trade art thou? Answer me directly.|
SAY:COBBLER:A trade, sir, that, I hope, I may use with a safe|
SAY:COBBLER:conscience; which is, indeed, sir, a mender of bad soles.|
SAY:MURELLUS:What trade, thou knave? Thou naughty knave, what trade?|
SAY:COBBLER:Nay, I beseech you, sir, be not out with me: yet,|
SAY:COBBLER:if you be out, sir, I can mend you.|
SAY:MURELLUS:What mean’st thou by that? Mend me, thou saucy fellow!|
SAY:COBBLER:Why, sir, cobble you.|
SAY:FLAVIUS:Thou art a cobbler, art thou?|
SAY:COBBLER:Truly, sir, all that I live by is with the awl. I meddle|
SAY:COBBLER:with no tradesman's matters, nor women’s matters, but|
SAY:COBBLER:with all. I am, indeed, sir, a surgeon to old shoes; when|
SAY:COBBLER:they are in great danger I recover them. As proper men|
SAY:COBBLER:as ever trod upon neat's leather have gone upon my|
SAY:COBBLER:handiwork.|
SAY:FLAVIUS:But wherefore art not in thy shop today?|
SAY:FLAVIUS:Why dost thou lead these men about the streets?|
SAY:COBBLER:Truly, sir, to wear out their shoes, to get myself|
SAY:COBBLER:into more work. But, indeed, sir, we make holiday, to see|
SAY:COBBLER:Caesar and to rejoice in his triumph.|
SAY:MURELLUS:Wherefore rejoice? What conquest brings he home?|
SAY:MURELLUS:What tributaries follow him to Rome,|
SAY:MURELLUS:To grace in captive bonds his chariot-wheels?|
SAY:MURELLUS:You blocks, you stones, you worse than senseless things!|
SAY:MURELLUS:O you hard hearts, you cruel men of Rome,|
SAY:MURELLUS:Knew you not Pompey? Many a time and oft|
SAY:MURELLUS:Have you climb'd up to walls and battlements,|
SAY:MURELLUS:To towers and windows, yea, to chimney-tops,|
SAY:MURELLUS:Your infants in your arms, and there have sat|
SAY:MURELLUS:The livelong day, with patient expectation,|
SAY:MURELLUS:To see great Pompey pass the streets of Rome.|
SAY:MURELLUS:And when you saw his chariot but appear,|
SAY:MURELLUS:Have you not made an universal shout,|
SAY:MURELLUS:That Tiber trembled underneath her banks,|
SAY:MURELLUS:To hear the replication of your sounds|
SAY:MURELLUS:Made in her concave shores?|
SAY:MURELLUS:And do you now put on your best attire?|
SAY:MURELLUS:And do you now cull out a holiday?|
SAY:MURELLUS:And do you now strew flowers in his way|
SAY:MURELLUS:That comes in triumph over Pompey's blood?|
SAY:MURELLUS:Be gone!|
SAY:MURELLUS:Run to your houses, fall upon your knees,|
SAY:MURELLUS:Pray to the gods to intermit the plague|
SAY:MURELLUS:That needs must light on this ingratitude.|
SAY:FLAVIUS:Go, go, good countrymen, and for this fault,|
SAY:FLAVIUS:Assemble all the poor men of your sort.|
SAY:FLAVIUS:Draw them to Tiber banks, and weep your tears|
SAY:FLAVIUS:Into the channel, till the lowest stream|
SAY:FLAVIUS:Do kiss the most exalted shores of all.|
SAY:FLAVIUS:See whe’er their basest mettle be not moved;|
SAY:FLAVIUS:They vanish tongue-tied in their guiltiness.|
SAY:FLAVIUS:Go you down that way towards the Capitol;|
SAY:FLAVIUS:This way will I. Disrobe the images,|
SAY:FLAVIUS:If you do find them decked with ceremonies.|
SAY:MURELLUS:May we do so?|
SAY:MURELLUS:You know it is the feast of Lupercal.|
SAY:FLAVIUS:It is no matter. Let no images|
SAY:FLAVIUS:Be hung with Caesar's trophies. I'll about,|
SAY:FLAVIUS:And drive away the vulgar from the streets.|
SAY:FLAVIUS:So do you too, where you perceive them thick.|
SAY:FLAVIUS:These growing feathers plucked from Caesar's wing|
SAY:FLAVIUS:Will make him fly an ordinary pitch,|
SAY:FLAVIUS:Who else would soar above the view of men|
SAY:FLAVIUS:And keep us all in servile fearfulness.|
